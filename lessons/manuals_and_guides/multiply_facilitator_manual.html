Section 1

Why we needed a method (and why this one)

A church that wants to make disciples needs more than good content. It needs a repeatable way for ordinary believers to lead other ordinary believers into transformation — without becoming professional teachers themselves.

For decades, most discipleship in Filipino churches has happened one of two ways: the sermon (one expert addressing many passive listeners) or the small-group study (well-intentioned but often improvised, depending heavily on the facilitator's natural gifting). Both produce some fruit. Neither scales.

What we needed was a method that:

  • Works in any setting — a coffee shop with one learner, a living room with five, a sanctuary with fifty
  • Requires no theological degree, only obedience and preparation
  • Treats the learner as an active participant, not a passive recipient
  • Moves people toward action, not just information
  • Sends them home with something to do, not just something to think about
  • Can be taught in one afternoon and refined over a lifetime

The 5-Movement Method is our answer. It draws from BTLI's adult-learning research (Hook-Book-Look-Took, ICAP, Knowles' andragogy), Mac Lake's leadership-pipeline thinking, and our own pastoral experience with what actually works in Filipino discipleship contexts. The Tagalog names — Pukaw, Tuklas, Talakay, Gawin, Dalhin sa Bahay — aren't translations of someone else's framework. They name what is actually happening in each part of a session, in language a Filipino disciple can feel.

"Information without engagement is forgotten. Engagement without action is entertainment. The 5 Movements exist to make sure every session moves a person from encounter to understanding to action to formation."
Section 2

What's actually different here

If you've been in church a while, none of the building blocks are new. Bible reading, discussion, application — every method uses these. So what is genuinely different about the 5-Movement Method?

The starting point is the learner, not the lesson. Most curricula begin with content ("today's topic is forgiveness"). PUKAW begins with the learner's life ("when was the last time you needed to forgive someone?"). The lesson doesn't arrive until the learner is ready to receive it.

Discovery comes before explanation. In TUKLAS, the facilitator doesn't tell people what the passage means. They guide the learner to discover it. Three discovery questions — observation, interpretation, application — replace the lecture.

Truth lands through story, not just doctrine. TALAKAY pairs the truth with a personal story. Not "here is the doctrine of God's love" but "here is a moment in my life when God's love became real to me — has anything like that happened to you?"

Every session ends in practice, not just learning. GAWIN asks: what will you actually do with this in the next 24 hours? Talking about evangelism without practicing it produces evangelists who never evangelize. Talking about prayer without praying produces prayers who never pray.

Formation continues after the session ends. DALHIN SA BAHAY is not a closing prayer — it is a sending. A specific assignment for the days between this session and the next. The session is one heartbeat in a longer rhythm.

🎯 The big idea
A MULTIPLY session is not a lesson delivered. It is a journey walked together through five movements — engaging, discovering, deepening, practicing, and sending. The facilitator is a guide, not a teacher.

⚡
Movement 1
PUKAW
"to awaken; to stir"
Purpose: Create a felt need. Surface a question your learner is already living with. Awaken curiosity for the truth they're about to encounter.
The rationale

The Bible was written for people with questions. When we open Scripture with someone who has no question, we are speaking to an empty room. PUKAW's job is to turn the lights on in the room — to surface a real human question that the upcoming Scripture passage will speak into.

This is why PUKAW never opens with "today's topic." A topic is a label. A question is a hook. We don't start with "today we'll study God's sovereignty." We start with: "Naranasan mo na ba na parang wala kang kontrol sa nangyayari sa buhay mo?"

The question is always personal, always concrete, never abstract.

Common formats
  • The contrast question: "What's the difference between X and Y?" — surfaces a distinction the lesson will deepen (Lesson 1 example: "alam tungkol sa" vs "kilala")
  • The memory question: "Remember a time when ___ happened to you" — anchors abstract truth in concrete experience
  • The observation prompt: "Look at this image / scene / object. What do you notice?" — opens with curiosity rather than statement
  • The honest paradox: "Why does X feel true even though we know Y is true?" — surfaces lived contradictions the lesson will address
✓ Do
Keep PUKAW short — 8 to 12 minutes only. Build felt need quickly, then transition. The temptation to linger here will sabotage the rest of the session.
✗ Don't
Lecture during PUKAW. The moment you start explaining, the felt need disappears. Let the silence after your question hang. Let them squirm a little.
📖
Movement 2
TUKLAS
"to discover; to find"
Purpose: Encounter Scripture together. Guide discovery, do not lecture. Let the text speak before you do.
The rationale

Most Filipinos have heard sermons their whole lives. What they have rarely experienced is discovering Scripture for themselves. When a learner says "naintindihan ko mismo from the verse, hindi kailangang sabihin sa akin" — formation has happened that no sermon can replicate.

TUKLAS uses three discovery questions in sequence:

  • 👁 OBSERVATION: "What does the text actually say?" — slows down rushed reading, surfaces details people glossed over
  • 💡 INTERPRETATION: "What does it mean?" — moves from text to truth without forcing the conclusion
  • 🙌 APPLICATION: "What does it mean for you?" — closes the loop back to the felt need from PUKAW

These three questions are not arbitrary — they map to the historical Christian practice of lectio divina (reading, meditation, prayer) and to inductive Bible study principles. They work because they honor how the mind actually processes meaning.

The Three-Reading method (small group)

For small groups, read the passage three times, three different voices:

  1. Facilitator reads aloud, slowly, in Tagalog
  2. One volunteer reads it again in English (or Tagalog if more comfortable)
  3. The whole group reads it together aloud

In 1-on-1, two readings is enough: facilitator reads once, then the learner reads it themselves in their own voice. Speaking the words changes how they land — read for understanding, not performance.

✓ Do
Let the learner answer. Even when their answer is partial or off. Then say "yes, and what about this part of the verse?" Guide them gently back to the text.
✗ Don't
Answer the questions yourself. If you give the answer, you have stolen the discovery. Better that they say "hindi ko alam" and you say "basahin natin uli ang verse" than you preach.
💡
Movement 3
TALAKAY
"to discuss; to deepen together"
Purpose: Make the truth personal. Pair the doctrine with a story. Give space for silence and reflection. Let the truth move from the head to the heart.
The rationale

If TUKLAS is the head, TALAKAY is the heart. The truth has been discovered; now it must be received. People rarely receive abstract truth — they receive stories that incarnate truth. Jesus knew this. His teaching was story upon story upon story.

TALAKAY has three sub-parts:

  • Part A — The Truth (stated plainly, ~3 min): Say the central truth of the lesson in one sentence. Don't add five more sentences explaining it. The simpler the statement, the more space it leaves for the Holy Spirit to do His own explaining.
  • Part B — The Story (~7-10 min): The facilitator shares a brief personal story where this truth became real for them. In small groups, 1-2 volunteers may also share. Not a sermon illustration — your own life.
  • Part C — Personal Reflection (~5-8 min of silence): The hardest and most important sub-part. The learner sits with the truth in silence. Writes in their participant page if they choose. The facilitator does not break the silence.
🎯 The silence is the lesson
Beginners always want to fill silence with chatter. Resist the urge. The 5-8 minutes of silence in TALAKAY is when the Holy Spirit does work that no facilitator can do. Sit at the table. Pray silently for your learner. Do not glance at your phone. The silence is not empty — it is full.
✓ Do
Tell your own story, briefly and specifically. Use the structure: Before / The Moment / After. Under 4 minutes. Vulnerability over polish.
✗ Don't
Use a famous testimony or someone else's story. "Si Pastor Rick Warren noong..." kills intimacy. They didn't come for Rick Warren. They came for you, and through you, for God.
🙌
Movement 4
GAWIN
"to do; to practice"
Purpose: Do something with the truth. Practice before leaving. Pray together. Surface one specific person to love or one specific action to take.
The rationale

James 1:22 — "Do not merely listen to the word, and so deceive yourselves. Do what it says."

Most discipleship in the Philippines stops at hearing. People nod, agree, feel moved — and walk out unchanged. GAWIN exists to break that pattern. Before the session ends, something must be done.

The "something" varies by lesson:

  • Practicum: A roleplay or rehearsal. Practice articulating your faith. Practice praying out loud. Practice forgiving someone in spoken words.
  • EOLO check-in: Each Each One Love One. Who is the one person in your life who doesn't yet know Jesus, that you are praying for and walking toward? GAWIN names them, prays for them, plans the next step.
  • Closing prayer practice: Especially in early Pre-Pipeline lessons, learners pray out loud — sometimes for the first time in their life.
✓ Do
Ask for ONE name, ONE action, ONE specific commitment. "Sino ang isang tao? Anong isang bagay?" — vague answers produce vague obedience.
✗ Don't
Let people answer in plurals. "My family, my coworkers..." is no one. Push for a name: "Sino specifically, sa lahat ng iyon?"
🏠
Movement 5
DALHIN SA BAHAY
"to bring home; to carry away"
Purpose: Send the learner home with something to carry. The 10-day assignment, the memory verse, the Pangako Ko commitment, the closing ritual — all done in 7-8 minutes.
The four elements
  1. The 10-day assignment: Always written, always specific, always feasible. For Pre-Pipeline: "One sentence in your journal each night for ten days." Not a prayer routine. Not a Bible reading plan. One sentence. Achievable. Habit-forming.
  2. The memory verse: One verse, recited twice in the session, screenshotted or written in the journal. Carried home in their pocket.
  3. Pangako Ko (My Commitment): A sentence frame the learner completes — "Sa susunod na dalawang linggo, isasabuhay ko ang katotohanang ___ sa pamamagitan ng ___." Specific. Achievable. Reviewable at the next session.
  4. The closing ritual: Always the same. Always sacred. (See Section 10.)
🌱 Why DALHIN is short, not long
A facilitator's instinct is to add more at the end — one more prayer, one more story, one more encouragement. Resist. DALHIN is short on purpose. The session ends with momentum, not exhaustion. The learner should leave wanting MORE, not feeling FULL.
Section 9

The session rhythm — pacing & energy

If you map the energy of a well-facilitated session, it looks like this:

  • PUKAW: Curious, slightly playful, slightly tense. "Why are you asking me this?"
  • TUKLAS: Engaged, leaning in, brain working. "Hmm, let me look at the verse again."
  • TALAKAY: Quiet, sober, sometimes tearful. "This is talking about me."
  • GAWIN: Slightly anxious, then committed. "Okay, I can try."
  • DALHIN: Settled, hopeful, sent. "I know what I'm doing this week."

If any of these emotional registers is missing from your session, something has gone wrong. The most common failures:

  • PUKAW is skipped or rushed → learner never builds felt need → TUKLAS lands as boring
  • TALAKAY's silence is broken → no heart movement → GAWIN becomes shallow
  • GAWIN's commitment is vague → DALHIN's assignment is forgotten

Each movement creates the conditions for the next. Skip one, and the rest weakens.

Section 10

What's sacred (do not change)

In a method built for flexibility, some things are still non-negotiable. Change anything else, but never these.

  1. The five movements, in order. Pukaw → Tuklas → Talakay → Gawin → Dalhin. Never start with TUKLAS to "save time." Never skip GAWIN because "we ran out." The order is the method.
  2. The closing declaration. Always: "Disciples who make disciples" (small group) or "Disciple ka na, kapatid. Magdi-disciple ka rin." (1-on-1). Said every session, the same way. Liturgy is how transformation enters the bones.
  3. The memory verse. Always reviewed. Always recited together at least once in the session. Always carried home.
  4. The Pangako Ko. One sentence. The learner fills the blanks themselves. Reviewed at the start of the next session.
  5. The silence in TALAKAY. The facilitator does not break it. Not with comfort, not with a follow-up question, not with "let's pray now." The Holy Spirit is working. Stay out of His way.
"Boredom is the price of liturgy. By Session 5, your learners will roll their eyes when you say 'Disciples who make disciples' for the fifth time. By Session 10, they will say it without you. By Session 20, they will say it to their own learners. Keep saying it."
Section 11

1-on-1 vs. small group — the two modes

Every Pre-Pipeline lesson is designed to be delivered in either of two modes: 1-on-1 (discipler + one learner) or small group (3-8 people). Plenary mode (50+) is reserved for BTLI-level training.

The facilitator HTML files have a mode toggle at the top. Tap "1-on-1" or "Small Group" before the session, and the file shows you the right activities, scripts, and timings for your context. Same lesson, same Scripture, same memory verse — different choreography.

When to use 1-on-1:

  • You are personally discipling someone who needs focused attention
  • The setting is intimate — coffee shop, home visit, walking conversation
  • The learner is processing something heavy and would shut down in a group
  • You want to model "each one love one" directly

When to use Small Group:

  • You are leading a Life Community group through Usbong as a cohort
  • Multiple seekers / new believers can grow together
  • You want learners to witness each other's transformation — formative by itself
  • Resources are limited — one facilitator can serve 8 instead of 1

Mode is a tactical choice, not a strategic one. Switch modes between sessions if your context changes. The lesson's spine stays the same.

Section 12

When sessions fail — and how to recover

No facilitator runs a perfect session. Here's what to do when things go sideways.

Failure 1 — Nobody answers PUKAW. The opening question hangs in the air; no one bites. Recovery: Don't rush to give an example. Wait three full seconds of silence. Then rephrase more concretely: "Let me ask it this way — when you were in college, may panahon ba na ___?" Specificity unlocks where abstraction freezes.

Failure 2 — Someone dominates the discussion. One person speaks for everyone, others quiet down. Recovery: Affirm them briefly — "That's a good point, Ate Maria" — then turn to someone else: "Kuya Joel, ano sa tingin mo?" Use names. Direct invitation breaks the dominance pattern without shaming.

Failure 3 — A learner asks a hard theological question you can't answer. "Bakit pinapayagan ng Diyos ang masama mangyari?" Recovery: Don't fake it. Say honestly: "Ang ganda ng tanong na 'yan. Hindi ko sigurado ang sagot — pero matatanong ko si Pastor at babalik tayo dito sa next session. Pwede ba tayong manatili sa text na binabasa natin muna?" Your honesty is more formative than any half-answer.

Failure 4 — The silence in TALAKAY breaks down. Someone giggles, someone fidgets, someone says "ang awkward!" Recovery: Smile gently, raise one hand a few inches off the table to signal "wait," and look down at your own Bible. Don't scold. Hold the space. The discomfort itself becomes part of the lesson.

Failure 5 — Someone cries. Tears come up unexpectedly during TALAKAY or GAWIN. Recovery: Pass them a tissue. Do not say "are you okay?" — they're not, and your asking will pressure them to compose themselves. Just say: "Take your time. Walang madali." Then keep going gently. Tears are sometimes the most accurate worship.

Failure 6 — The session runs over time. You're past 80 minutes and still in GAWIN. Recovery: Cut DALHIN to its bones — just the assignment + memory verse + Pangako + closing ritual. Skip extra encouragement. Honor the original time commitment. Going over time once is fine; making it a pattern erodes trust.

Section 13

A word to the facilitator

If you have read this far, you are taking up something significant. A few words, kapatid.

You are not a teacher. You are a fellow disciple who has walked one step further down the path than the person sitting across from you. That step is enough.

You do not need to be eloquent. The 5 Movements are designed for ordinary people. The facilitator file gives you the script when you freeze. The Scripture passage carries the weight you cannot. The Holy Spirit does what neither of you can do.

You will be tempted to over-prepare — to study commentaries, memorize doctrines, plan elaborate transitions. Resist. The best facilitators are fully present, not maximally informed. Read the lesson the night before. Pray for your learner by name. Show up early. Listen more than you speak.

You will be tempted to compare yourself to other facilitators who seem more natural, more articulate, more spiritual. Resist this too. Your learner did not come for someone else. They came for you, with your specific story, your specific limitations, your specific Christ-formed life. That is enough.

You will sometimes feel like nothing happened. The session was flat. The discussion was thin. The silence felt empty. Trust the method. Formation does not always announce itself. Seeds dropped in soil look like nothing for weeks. The 10th session, looking back at the 1st, is when you'll see what God was doing all along.

"Luke 6:40 — 'Every disciple, when fully trained, will become like his teacher.' You are not training apprentices to know what you know. You are training them to become who you are becoming. The 5 Movements are simply the path you walk together, side by side, toward Christ."
