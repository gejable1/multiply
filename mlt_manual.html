Chapter 1 Creating & Managing Batches

A batch is a small class of disciples taking the same lesson together. This chapter shows you how to create one, enroll your members, and care for the group from start to finish.

For new readers

This manual is for LC Leaders (LCLs). If a word is new to you — like batch, cohort, or pipeline level — check the Glossary at the end. Don't worry, kapatid, this gets easy with practice.

1. What is a batch, and when do I need one?

Think of your LC Group (LCG) as your whole flock — every disciple God has entrusted to you. Now think of a batch as a small classroom — a few people studying the same lesson together over weeks or months.

Your LCG might have 12 members. Of those 12, maybe 5 are ready for the BTLI 1 class that starts this month. Those 5 become a batch. The other 7 stay in your LCG but aren't in this particular class.

The difference at a glance

  • LC Group = everyone you shepherd. Big and ongoing.
  • Batch = a specific class for a specific lesson. Small and time-bound.

When do you need to create a batch?

Decision tree: Do I need a batch?

Examples of when you'd create a batch:

  • You're teaching 4 of your disciples a "Pre-believer's Class" every Monday at 5 PM.
  • You have a group of new converts doing daily devotionals together, and you meet them weekly.
  • You're running a small reading group for one of the Mac Lake books.

You do NOT need a batch for:

  • Your regular weekly LC meeting where anyone in your LCG might show up. Just use Attendance directly.
  • Sunday Service. That's a church-wide event, not a class.
  • One-on-one mentoring. Just disciple them — no batch needed.

2. Who can do what

Two kinds of batches exist, and your permissions depend on which kind it is:

  • Pastor-owned batches — Pastor created them. Examples: official BTLI 1 cohorts, church-wide EOLO training.
  • Your own batches — You (the LCL) created them. Example: your Monday 5 PM Pre-Believer's group.

4. How to create your own batch

Create a batch when you need a small classroom for your own disciples — usually a regular meeting where you'll teach a specific curriculum over weeks or months.

Flow: Creating a batch

Step-by-step

  1. Open My Batches

    From the MLT home screen, tap the 🎓 My Batches card under Quick Actions.

  2. Scroll down and tap "+ Create my own batch"

    At the bottom of the Batches list, you'll see a dashed-bordered button. Tap it to open the form.

  3. Fill in the batch name

    Pick a name your disciples will recognize. Good examples: "Monday 5PM", "Saturday 7am Group", "MARK Disciples". Bad example: "Batch 1" (too generic — confusing later).

  4. Choose a program

    The program is the curriculum your batch will follow. Pastor sets up the available programs. If you're not sure which one to pick, ask Pastor before saving — the wrong program means the wrong lesson materials.

  5. (Optional) Set start and end dates, and notes

    Dates help with planning but aren't required. Notes are for context like "Meets at the church library, bring your Bible."

  6. Tap "💾 Create"

    You'll see a green toast: "✓ Batch created". The new batch now appears under "My Batches" with the gold My Batch pill.

Heads-up

You can only create batches under programs Pastor has already set up. If the program you need doesn't exist yet, talk to Pastor — they'll add it to the system.

5. How to enroll a disciple into a batch

Whether it's your batch or one of Pastor's, the enrollment process is the same. The system makes sure only eligible members show up in the picker, so you can't accidentally add the wrong person.

Flow: Enrolling a disciple

Step-by-step

  1. Open the batch you want to enroll into

    From the Batches screen, find the batch (under "Available Batches" if Pastor's, or "My Batches" if yours). Tap Manage.

  2. Tap "+ Add member"

    You'll see the current roster (might be empty). The "+ Add member" button is at the bottom of the screen.

  3. Find the disciple you want to enroll

    The picker shows your eligible members. You can search by typing a name. Each row shows their pipeline level and LC group.

  4. Tap "+ Enroll" next to their name

    A green toast confirms: "✓ [Name] enrolled". Their name disappears from the picker (they're now in the batch) so you can keep enrolling others without confusion.

Why some disciples don't appear in the picker

If you can't find someone in the picker, there are three possible reasons:

  • Their pipeline level doesn't match the program. If the batch is for an L1 program, only L1 disciples appear. An L0 disciple (new convert who hasn't been commissioned to L1 yet) won't show up. Talk to Pastor about whether they should be commissioned, or whether the program is right for them.
  • They're already enrolled in another batch of the same program. A member can only be in one active batch per program at a time. If your disciple is already in BTLI 1 · Batch A, they won't appear when you try to add them to BTLI 1 · Batch B.
  • They're not in your LeaderScope. You can only enroll members you actually shepherd (your tree of disciples). If someone is outside your scope, the system won't show them.
Pastor's batches are different

If you're enrolling into a Pastor-owned batch, the same rules apply — but the batch belongs to Pastor. You can add and withdraw your disciples, but the batch itself (name, program, graduation) stays under Pastor's care.

6. How to withdraw a disciple

Sometimes a disciple needs to leave a batch — maybe they switched schedules, moved away, or weren't ready. Withdrawing is the honest, gentle way to take them out without erasing their history.

  1. Open the batch

    From Batches → tap Manage on the relevant batch.

  2. Find the disciple in the Enrolled list

    Each enrolled member has a Withdraw button on their row.

  3. Tap "Withdraw"

    You'll see a confirmation: "Withdraw [Name] from this batch?" with a reminder that their attendance history is kept.

  4. Confirm

    The system marks them as "withdrew" with today's date. They move from the active roster to a History section at the bottom of the batch view.

Pastoral note

Withdrawing someone is not a failure — it's an honest accounting. Don't hide a disciple's struggle; record it gently and follow up with them outside the batch. The system keeps their history so Pastor and you can shepherd them well.

7. How to graduate a batch

When everyone in your batch has finished the curriculum, you graduate the whole batch together. This is a one-way celebration step — it marks the end and (for now) cannot be undone.

  1. Make sure everyone is ready

    Graduating means everyone in the active roster gets marked as graduated at once. If someone isn't ready, withdraw them first or talk to Pastor.

  2. Open the batch and tap "🎓 Graduate all"

    The button appears in the bottom action bar (only for batches you can graduate — see permissions table).

  3. Confirm the celebration

    You'll see: "Graduate all [N] active member(s) of '[Batch Name]'? This is a one-way celebration step." Tap OK to confirm.

  4. Celebrate, kapatid!

    A toast appears: "🎓 Batch graduated. Congratulations!" The batch status changes to "graduated" and all active members get their status changed to "graduated" too.

Pastor-owned batches

You cannot graduate Pastor-owned batches like BTLI 1. Graduation from official programs is Pastor's act of commissioning — it has spiritual weight, not just an admin button. You'll see the message "Pastor-owned program — Pastor graduates" if you try.

8. How batches connect to attendance

Batches aren't just for tracking enrollment — they shape who appears in the attendance list for BTLI sessions.

When you log attendance for a BTLI lesson, the system asks you which batch the session is for. Once you pick the batch, the attendance roster shows only members enrolled in that batch — not your whole LC group. This means non-enrolled members can't be accidentally marked absent for a class they're not in.

Non-BTLI events (Sunday Service, regular LC meetings) still use your whole LCG. Only BTLI events use the batch roster.

In short

A clean batch roster = clean attendance reports. Keep your enrollments honest and the rest of the system stays honest too.

9. Common mistakes & how to fix them

"I created a batch but used the wrong program"

Open the batch in the Batches admin (or ask Pastor if you can't edit it). Currently, the LCL self-edit doesn't let you change the program of an existing batch — the simplest fix is to graduate or close the wrong batch and create a new one with the right program. Re-enroll members into the new batch.

"I enrolled the wrong person"

Just withdraw them. The Withdraw button works for any enrollment you have rights to manage. No harm done; their record is preserved as "withdrew" for honesty.

"My disciple doesn't appear in the picker but I'm sure they should"

Check three things:

  1. Their pipeline level — does it match the program?
  2. Are they already in another batch of the same program?
  3. Are they actually in your discipleship tree? (Check My Group → Tree view)

"I accidentally graduated my batch too early"

Graduation is currently one-way. Talk to Pastor — they can fix it in the dashboard, but it requires admin help. This is intentional friction so graduations aren't taken lightly.

"Pastor created a batch but I don't see it"

Three possible reasons:

  • The batch status is "graduated" or "closed" — you only see active and forming batches.
  • The batch's program has a pipeline level that doesn't match any of your scoped members — you'd technically see it, but it's filtered. Refresh the screen.
  • The batch was just created and your phone has a cached older version — pull down to refresh, or restart the app.

10. When to ask Pastor

The system is designed so you can do most things on your own. But these are moments where it's right to bring Pastor in:

  • You need a new program (curriculum) — Pastor sets up the program; you create batches under it.
  • A member needs their pipeline level changed — Pastor handles commissioning. Don't try to work around the level-match rule by asking Pastor to enroll someone manually; the rule exists for a reason.
  • A graduation needs to be undone — Pastor's admin tools can correct it.
  • A BTLI batch needs to graduate — Pastor's call.
  • You're not sure if a disciple is ready — That's a pastoral conversation, not a system question.
A final word, kapatid

This tool is here to support your shepherding, not replace it. The numbers and rosters help you keep track, but the people behind them matter most. May God bless your faithfulness in this work — Luke 6:40 reminds us that a well-trained disciple becomes like their teacher. Let your training reflect Christ.

Chapter 2 Starting Usbong Lessons with Your Pre-Pipeline Disciples

Usbong is the very first set of lessons for a new disciple — someone still getting to know Jesus (we call them Pre-Pipeline). This chapter shows you, step by step, how to open these lessons for your disciples. By the end, you'll know how to do the whole thing on your own.

Think of it like this

You are a teacher offering a new seminar. The seminar is called a batch. You give it a clear name, you sign up your students, and then you start the first lesson. That's the whole idea — everything below is just the buttons you press to do it.

1. The big picture — five steps from start to finish

Before the details, here is the whole journey on one screen. Don't worry about understanding every box yet — we'll walk through each one slowly.

Flow: From zero to your disciples taking Usbong
Before you start — one thing to check

Make sure your disciples already have MMT (the Member Tool) installed on their phones. That's the app where they'll see and take the lessons. If they don't have it yet, help them install it first — Step 5 won't work without it. (See Section 6 below.)

2. Step 1 & 2 — Open a batch and name it the right way

Opening a batch is exactly like the previous chapter (Creating & Managing Batches). The only special thing for Usbong is how you name it. Please read this part slowly — a clear name saves everyone confusion later.

The naming rule (please follow this exactly)

Your batch name must have three parts, in this order:

The batch-name formula

So the pattern is: Your name → the word "Usbong" → a short label that helps you spot this batch among others.

Good names vs. confusing names

✓ Good: Maria Usbong 2026  ·  Maria Usbong AM Batch  ·  Maria Usbong Saturday

✗ Confusing: Usbong (whose?)  ·  Batch 1 (no name, no "Usbong")  ·  Maria's Group (which course?)

Why your name AND "Usbong" are both required

When the church has 48 LC Leaders, there will be many batches. If everyone just writes "Usbong," nobody can tell which batch is whose. Your name says it's yours. "Usbong" says which course it teaches. The third label (year, time, day) helps when you run more than one Usbong batch over the years.

Step-by-step (opening + naming)

  1. Tap "🎓 My Batches" on your MLT home screen

    This opens the Batches screen — your list of seminars.

  2. Scroll down and tap "+ Create my own batch"

    It's the dashed button at the bottom. This opens the new-batch form.

  3. Type the batch name using the formula

    Example: Maria Usbong 2026. Your name, then "Usbong," then your label.

  4. Choose the program: "New & Pre-believer's Class" (Usbong)

    This is the curriculum your batch will follow. If you're not sure which program is the Usbong one, ask Pastor before saving — the wrong program means the wrong lessons.

  5. Tap "💾 Create"

    A green message says "✓ Batch created." Your new batch now appears under "My Batches" with a gold My Batch pill. 🎉

3. Step 3 — Add your disciples (your students)

Now you invite your students into the seminar. The system is smart: it only shows people you actually shepherd and who are at the right level, so you can't add the wrong person by accident.

  1. Open your batch and tap "Roster"

    The roster is the list of students. It might be empty at first — that's normal.

  2. Tap "+ Add member"

    A picker opens showing your eligible disciples. You can type a name to search.

  3. Pick the disciple and tap "+ Add" (role: participant)

    Leave the role as participant — that's the normal setting for a student. (Teacher and apprentice are for people helping you teach.)

  4. Repeat for each disciple

    Each added name disappears from the picker so you don't add the same person twice. Keep going until everyone is in.

Can't find your disciple in the list?

Usually it's because they're at the wrong level for this course, they're already in another Usbong batch, or they're not in your group yet. The previous chapter explains all three reasons under "Why some disciples don't appear in the picker." When in doubt, ask Pastor.

4. Step 4 — Open the lessons to the batch

Here's the important idea: adding a student does not open a lesson yet. Think of it like a seminar where the students are seated, but you haven't handed out the first module. You open lessons one at a time, when your batch is ready for each one. This keeps your students focused — they won't jump ahead to Lesson 5 while you're still teaching Lesson 1.

Flow: Opening a lesson (today, and in the future)
For now: ask Pastor

Today, opening (unlocking) a lesson is done by Pastor. Just send a quick message: "Pastor, please open Lesson 1 for my batch Maria Usbong 2026." Pastor opens it, and within moments your students can see Lesson 1 in their MMT.

Where we're headed

With 48 LC Leaders, Pastor can't open every lesson for everyone forever — and he shouldn't have to. Soon, you'll open lessons yourself straight from your batch screen, one lesson at a time, when your group is ready. Same idea, just faster. This manual will be updated when that's ready.

One lesson at a time — on purpose

You open Lesson 1 first. When your batch finishes it and is ready for Lesson 2, you (or Pastor, for now) open Lesson 2 — and so on. Until a lesson is opened, your students will see it as 🔒 Locked with the words "Opens when your facilitator releases this lesson." That's normal and good — it means the system is keeping your class together.

5. Step 5 — Your disciples need MMT on their phones

Everything above prepares the lessons. But your students see and take the lessons inside MMT (the Member Tool) on their own phones. No MMT, no lessons. So this step is about them, not you.

  1. Make sure each disciple has MMT installed

    If they don't have it yet, help them install it (add it to their phone's home screen like an app). It only takes a minute.

  2. They log in as themselves

    MMT is their personal member view — different from your leader tool. They sign in with their own member login.

  3. They tap "Journey" at the bottom

    That's where the Usbong lessons and quizzes live.

6. Step 6 — Using the lessons after everything is set up

Now you teach! Here's the simple weekly rhythm once a lesson is open:

  1. You teach the lesson in your meeting

    Gather your batch (in person or online) and go through the lesson together, the way you normally disciple.

  2. You log attendance for that meeting

    Mark who came, choosing event type Pre-Pipeline and picking the lesson (e.g. "Usbong 1 · L1"). This is covered in detail in the Attendance chapter. Attendance also helps unlock the quiz for anyone who attended.

  3. Your students take the lesson's quiz in MMT

    After the lesson is open, they tap Take Quiz on that lesson. It's 10 questions, best of 3 tries. It just checks what they remembered — gentle, not scary.

  4. When the batch is ready, open the next lesson

    Ask Pastor to open Lesson 2 (soon you'll do this yourself). Repeat the rhythm: teach → log attendance → quiz → open next.

A simple way to remember the weekly loop

Teach → Attendance → Quiz → Open the next. Four small steps, repeated for each lesson, until your disciples finish Usbong and are ready for the next stage of their journey.

7. Things people often ask (and small mistakes to avoid)

"My student says ALL the lessons are open — is that wrong?"

For a participant (a normal student), only the lessons you've opened should be takeable; the rest should show 🔒 Locked. If a student sees everything open, check two things: (1) Was the student accidentally added as a teacher or apprentice? Those helper roles can see all lessons on purpose. (2) If they're a plain participant and still see all lessons open, tell Pastor — that's a setup issue to look into.

"I added my student but they still can't see Lesson 1."

Adding a student and opening a lesson are two separate steps. After you add them (Step 3), the lesson still needs to be opened (Step 4). Ask Pastor to open Lesson 1, then have them refresh their MMT.

"Should I open all 10 lessons at once to save time?"

Please don't. Open one lesson at a time as your batch is ready. Opening everything at once lets students race ahead alone and skips the togetherness that makes discipleship work. Slow and together beats fast and scattered.

"What if a student joins late?"

Just add them to the batch (Step 3). Whatever lessons are already open, they'll see open too. Catch them up pastorally — the system won't punish a late joiner.

A final word, kapatid

Usbong is where a new believer takes their first real steps. You're not just unlocking lessons — you're walking beside someone as they meet Jesus. The buttons are easy; the relationship is the real work. Go gently, celebrate every small win, and remember: a fully-trained disciple becomes like their teacher (Luke 6:40). May your patience reflect His.

Chapter 3 · Part 1 Attendance

Two people can witness the same meeting: the member who was there, and the leader who watched. MULTIPLY lets both speak. This part of the chapter explains the two ways attendance gets logged, when to use each, and how confirmations flow.

A pastoral note before we start

Attendance is not surveillance, kapatid. It is shepherding made visible. When you log who showed up, you're not catching people out — you're noticing who's growing, who's drifting, and who needs a phone call this week. Read this chapter with that lens.

1. The big picture: two ways to log attendance

In most churches, only the leader records who came. MULTIPLY does it differently: the member can also say "I was here," and the leader confirms it. Two witnesses, one record.

This mirrors a biblical pattern. "Every matter must be established by the testimony of two or three witnesses" (2 Cor 13:1). Self-attestation respects the member's dignity. Confirmation preserves your role as shepherd. Together they produce an attendance record that's both gentle and honest.

The two flows side by side

The two parallel attendance flows

Both flows write to the same attendance table. Reports treat a confirmed self-attest the same as an LCL-reported row. The two flows aren't competing — they're complementary, each covering what the other can't see.

2. When to use LCL-reported attendance

This is the default. Most attendance gets logged this way, because you, the LCL, are the natural witness for your group's meetings.

Use LCL-reported attendance when:

  • You're physically at the event — Sunday Service, your weekly LC meeting, a BTLI lesson you're teaching. You see who showed up. Log it.
  • The event is for everyone in your LCG — your regular meetings where the whole group is expected. The roster is fixed (your LC group); you're just marking who came.
  • The event is more than 7 days old — members can only self-attest within the last 7 days. For older meetings, only you can backfill (or talk to Pastor for very old corrections).
  • You need to record absences explicitly — self-attest only creates "present" rows. If you need a clean, complete record where everyone is marked either present or absent, you must log it yourself.
  • It's a BTLI session — BTLI attendance has special requirements (the batch picker, lesson selection). Always LCL-logged from MLT.
Quick mental check

Ask yourself: "Was I there, watching the door?" If yes → LCL-reported is the right path. If no → self-attest is probably the right path (see next section).

What LCL-reported attendance produces

  • One row per member-event pair (your whole group or batch).
  • Each row marked either present or absent.
  • source is blank (default), meaning "logged by leader."
  • confirmed_by is blank (no confirmation needed — you are the witness).
  • The system trusts your record as final unless someone disputes it through Pastor.

3. When to use member self-attestation

Self-attest exists for events where you weren't there to witness, but the member was. It dignifies the member's testimony while keeping you in the accountability loop through the confirmation step.

Members should self-attest when:

  • They attended a service or event you weren't at — they visited another LCG, attended a church event you missed, joined a special prayer meeting, etc.
  • The event isn't on your usual schedule — a one-off conference, a guest speaker night, a retreat you didn't run.
  • They arrived late or you missed marking them — instead of you backfilling later, they can self-attest the same day.
  • You're sick or unavailable that week — life happens. Members can still record their faithfulness; your confirmation can come later.
When NOT to use self-attest

Self-attest is not for events you would normally log yourself. Don't ask members to self-attest for your weekly LCG meeting just to save you time — that breaks the two-witness model and removes useful accountability. Self-attest is the exception path, not a shortcut.

This card on your MLT home screen shows self-attest rows from your direct disciples that need your action. It's the single most important thing to understand for the two-witness model to work.

What you're seeing

  • Member name — who self-attested.
  • Event and date — what they say they attended.
  • "2× nudged" badge — appears when a member has reminded you twice or more. It's their way of gently saying "please look at this." Don't ignore it.
  • ✓ Confirm button — you saw them, or you trust their report. One tap, done.
  • ✕ Dispute button — you have good reason to believe they weren't actually there. Opens a quick reason picker.

Three honest things to know about the Pending Confirmations card

It only shows YOUR direct disciples

The card only lists self-attest rows from members whose discipler_id is you. Not your full tree — just your direct disciples. This is intentional: confirmation is a direct-discipler responsibility, not a group of cascading approvals. If your disciple Pedro disciples someone named Juan, Juan's self-attests appear on Pedro's screen, not yours.

It refreshes every 90 seconds

You don't have to manually reload. New self-attestations appear within a minute and a half of being submitted. The badge on the card shows the count.

Disputes go to Pastor, not into a black hole

When you tap Dispute, the row isn't deleted. It's marked as disputed with your reason, and Pastor sees it in their dispute queue. Pastor decides what counts. This protects both you and the member from awkward direct conflict — you don't have to argue with a disciple; you escalate gently.

How to confirm

  1. Open MLT

    The Pending Confirmations card appears at the top of your home screen if there's anything to act on. If it's not there, you have no pending items.

  2. Read the row

    Check the member, event, and date. Ask yourself: "Do I believe this was true?" Most of the time the answer is yes — your disciples aren't trying to fool you.

  3. Tap "✓ Confirm"

    A confirmation dialog asks: "Confirm this member was actually present?" Tap OK. The row disappears from your pending list (it's now confirmed) and the member sees their attendance status as confirmed.

How to dispute

  1. Tap "✕ Dispute"

    A modal opens with three preset reasons plus a custom field.

  2. Pick the reason

    The three preset reasons are:

    • 🚫 Wasn't actually there — they weren't physically present.
    • 📅 Came to a different service/date — they were at something, but not this event.
    • ⏰ Came too late to count — they showed up after the cutoff.

    Or type a custom reason if none of these fit. Be specific but kind — Pastor will read this.

  3. Submit

    The row is marked disputed with your reason and escalates to Pastor's queue. You get a toast: "✕ Dispute logged. Pastor will review."

A word on disputes

A dispute is not a punishment, kapatid. It's a request for a third witness when two don't agree. Use it sparingly — most "discrepancies" are just memory differences or misunderstandings. Talk to the member first if you can. Save formal disputes for cases where the record genuinely matters and you genuinely can't agree.

Chapter 3 · Part 2 Attendance — The Mechanics

5. Logging LCG attendance, step by step

This is the bread-and-butter flow — the one you'll use after every LC meeting, Sunday service, prayer meeting, or outing. The screen is the same; only the event type pill changes.

The actual flow

  1. Open MLT and tap "✅ Log Attendance"

    The home screen has a Quick Action card for it. The attendance screen opens with today's date pre-filled and Sunday Service pre-selected as the default event type.

  2. Adjust the date (if needed)

    If you're logging today's meeting, leave the date alone. If you forgot last Thursday's LC night and you're catching up, tap the date field and pick the correct date. There's no "too late" — you can log attendance for any date, past or present.

    Tip: Don't tap a future date by accident. The system will let you, but the row will read as "Pedro attended a meeting that hasn't happened yet" — which is weird in reports.

  3. Pick the event type

    Tap one of the pills: Sunday Service, LC Meeting, Sunday School, Prayer Meeting, BTLI, Youth Gathering, Team Building, Outing, or Other. The selected one turns gold.

    For BTLI, two extra pickers appear (lesson and batch) — see Section 6.

  4. Watch for the green "Already saved" banner

    If someone (you, a co-leader, or Pastor) already logged this date + event type, a green banner appears showing who logged it and how many were present. The checkboxes will already be filled in to match. You're now editing an existing record, not creating a new one. If everything looks right, you don't even need to re-tap Save — but doing so doesn't hurt.

    If you don't see the banner, this is a fresh entry.

  5. Tap the checkbox next to each member who was present

    The list is your LCG (your shepherding tree). Tap a row anywhere — the whole row is the tap target, not just the tiny box. A green checkmark appears; the row brightens. Tap again to uncheck.

    If most members came, tap Select All at the bottom, then uncheck only the absentees. Faster.

  6. Tap "💾 Save"

    The footer counter says "X present" so you can sanity-check before saving. Tap Save. A green toast appears: "✅ Saved! 6/8 present". The screen auto-returns to home after 1.5 seconds.

Friendly tip — the "Already saved" banner is your friend

Two leaders logging the same meeting? No problem. The second one to open the screen sees the green banner, sees who logged it, and can either add a missed name or close the screen. The system never creates duplicates for the same member + date + event_type — it just updates the existing row.

What if my phone died mid-attendance?

MULTIPLY auto-saves your checks to your phone's local storage every time you tap. If the tab gets killed (Android Chrome does this when memory is tight, especially when you switch to WhatsApp), the next time you open Attendance, you'll see an orange recovery banner:

↩ Recovered 5 unsaved checks from 12 mins ago. Review and tap Save when ready.

Your checkboxes are already restored. Just review, fix anything that's wrong, and tap Save. Drafts older than 24 hours are quietly discarded — those are almost always abandoned, not recoverable work.

6. Logging BTLI attendance with the batch picker

BTLI attendance is similar to LCG attendance, but with two extra steps that make sure the right people get credit for being at the right lesson with the right batch. These two extras matter, kapatid — without them, the comprehension quiz can't unlock for your members.

The two extra pickers

When you select BTLI as the event type, two gold-bordered cards appear under the event type pills:

  1. 📚 Which BTLI Lesson? — picks the specific lesson (e.g., "BTLI 1 · L1 · Pananalangin"). This unlocks the comprehension quiz for everyone you mark present.
  2. 🎓 Which Batch? — picks which of your active batches met. This narrows the roster to only the enrolled members, so non-enrolled LCG members can't be falsely marked absent for a class they're not in.

The BTLI flow (the parts that differ from Section 5)

  1. Pick the date

    Same as before. Today, or whenever the BTLI session actually happened.

  2. Tap the "BTLI" event type pill

    Two gold-bordered cards slide in below the pills: 📚 Which BTLI Lesson? and (after you pick a lesson) 🎓 Which Batch?

  3. Pick the lesson from the dropdown

    The dropdown lists every active BTLI lesson across all BTLI courses (BTLI 1, BTLI 2, etc.). Tap the one you just taught — for example, "BTLI 1 · L1 · Pananalangin."

    The system uses this to construct the event_name on the attendance row, which is how the comprehension quiz knows whether to unlock for each member.

  4. Pick the batch from the dropdown

    Now the batch dropdown is populated. It shows every active batch that teaches this course — including Pastor-owned batches you can see and your own batches if you have any. Tap the batch that just met.

    The roster narrows. Instead of your full LCG, you now see only the members enrolled in this batch.

  5. Mark attendance and save

    From here it's identical to LCG attendance: tap checkboxes, optionally Select All, tap Save.

If you skip the batch pick, Save will refuse

MULTIPLY won't let you save BTLI attendance without both a lesson and a batch chosen. If you tap Save without one of them, you'll get a red toast — "Please pick the batch before saving BTLI attendance." — and the missing dropdown will get a focus ring. This is intentional. Without these two, the quiz can't unlock and non-enrolled members might get false absences.

The decision the system makes for you

What happens when you pick BTLI

Why this matters for the comprehension quiz

Every BTLI lesson has a comprehension quiz that members take from MMT. The quiz is gated — locked until the member is either enrolled in an active batch of that course (the cohort path) or has an attendance row matching that lesson (the fallback path).

When you correctly log BTLI attendance with the lesson picker, the attendance row gets the right event_name — and the moment Pedro opens MMT after class, his quiz card flips from 🔒 LOCKED to 📖 NOT YET TAKEN. He can start the quiz that same afternoon if he wants. That's the whole point of getting the picker right.

7. Ministry attendance (for ministry heads)

If you're a ministry head — worship team, ushering, multimedia, kids' ministry, etc. — there's a separate attendance flow built around the team you lead, not your LCG. Most LCLs don't need to read this section. If you are a head, here's what's different.

Where to find it

From your MLT home screen, look for the 🎵 Ministry Attendance quick action. (The icon may differ per ministry — kids', tech, ushering, etc.) It opens a separate screen from the LCG attendance screen.

What's different about ministry attendance

  • The roster is your ministry team, not your LCG. People who serve in multiple ministries show up only on the ministries they actually serve in.
  • Optional sub-roster picker. If your ministry has formal sub-rosters (e.g., "Worship Team A" vs. "Worship Team B" alternating Sundays), pick the one that just served. Leave it blank to see the whole ministry team.
  • Different event types. Ministry Practice, Ministry Rehearsal, Ministry Meeting, Ministry Service, Other. Pick the one that fits.
  • Walk-ins are supported. When a substitute fills in or a guest helps out, tap "+ Add walk-in / substitute" at the bottom of the list. You'll get a person picker. The walk-in gets attendance credit for this specific event but is not permanently added to the ministry roster — perfect for one-off substitutions.
  • You can pull a report. The "📊 View Report" button at the bottom of the screen takes you to a ministry-scoped attendance report for the people you lead.
Two hats, two screens

If you're both an LCL and a ministry head, log your meetings in the LCG attendance screen and your ministry practices in the Ministry Attendance screen. Same person, different scopes — the reports will read both correctly because of the event types.

8. Edge cases and common pitfalls

Most days, attendance is a 90-second tap-and-go. But here are the situations where things get a little tricky — and what to do about each one.

"I logged the wrong date / event type — how do I fix it?"

Two ways, depending on what's wrong:

  • Wrong date: Re-open the attendance screen, switch to the correct date, log it again, and ask Pastor to delete the wrong-date row via the Attendance Admin tool (Section 10).
  • Wrong event type (e.g., you marked Sunday Service as LC Meeting): Same — log it again under the correct type, then ask Pastor to clean up the wrong one.

LCLs can't delete attendance rows directly. This is by design — the act of deletion needs a Pastor's hand on it, both for trust and for the rare cases where a row has been confirmed through self-attest (Section 10).

"Two leaders logged the same meeting — did we make duplicates?"

No, kapatid. The system enforces a uniqueness rule: for a given member + date + event_type (plus event_name for BTLI), there can only be one row. The second leader to save sees the green "Already saved" banner, the checkboxes pre-fill to match what's on file, and any change they make updates the existing row rather than creating a new one. The logged_by field shows whoever saved most recently.

"A member self-attested but I'm sure they weren't there"

Part 1, Section 4 covers this in full. Short version: tap ✕ Dispute in the Pending Confirmations card on your MLT home screen, pick a reason, submit. The row escalates to Pastor's queue. Don't argue with the member directly if you can avoid it — escalation is gentler.

"My phone closed while I was taking attendance"

Re-open MLT → Attendance. The orange recovery banner restores your unsaved checks (see Section 5). For BTLI specifically, the lesson and batch picks don't persist in the draft — you'll need to re-pick them, then your checkboxes come back. Annoying but not data-corrupting. Save guards catch any missing fields.

"Someone I disciple came but they're not in my LCG yet"

You can't mark someone present if they're not in your roster — they have to be added as your disciple first. Two options: (a) go to Members → + Add Member, add them, then return to attendance; or (b) for one-off visitors, just note them on paper and let Pastor know. Don't try to backfill weeks of attendance for newly added members — the cleanest practice is forward-going from the day they join your LCG.

"I switched event type mid-flow — did I lose my checks?"

Yes, switching event type clears the current checks because the meaning changes. The system reloads from the database (if a row exists for the new type) or starts blank. If you accidentally tapped the wrong pill, don't worry — your draft is still on the phone for the previous event type. Switch back and you'll see the recovery banner.

"I marked everyone present by accident with Select All"

Tap Select All a second time — it toggles. If everyone is currently checked, it unchecks everyone. Then re-check the actual attendees.

When in doubt, save

Almost any mistake is fixable through Pastor's admin tool or by re-saving. The one thing that's not fixable is the attendance you never recorded because you weren't sure. So when you're uncertain, save anyway — Pastor can clean it up. Missing data is worse than messy data, kapatid.

9. From attendance to insight — where does this data go?

You're not just feeding a database, kapatid. Every checkbox you tap rolls up into reports that shape pastoral care across the church. Here's where to find what you've logged.

The LC Attendance Report

From MLT home, tap 📊 Reports → LC Attendance Report. This shows your LCG's attendance over the last 8 weeks, sorted into three tiers:

  • 🏆 Champions Circle — members who showed up 7 or 8 of the last 8 weeks. Encourage them; they're modeling faithfulness.
  • 🌱 Faithfully Gathering — 4 to 6 of 8. Steady, but worth checking in if you see a recent drop.
  • 💔 Awaiting Your Heartbeat — 3 or fewer. These are your phone-call list this week. Not "absent record" — these are names of sheep who haven't been seen lately. Pick one, call them, ask how they are. That's the whole point.

What the report keys off

The report uses your LCG roster (your shepherding tree), not the sparse lc_group text field. So even if Maria moved LCGs last month, the report follows her to her new shepherd — she's not double-counted anywhere.

BTLI attendance is separate

BTLI attendance doesn't roll into the LC Attendance Report (those are class days, not community gathering days). It powers the BTLI Quiz unlock and Course Grade screens instead, and Pastor can pull a course-level attendance view from the dashboard.

10. What Pastor can do that you can't — the Attendance Admin tool

Pastor has a separate admin page that no LCL can access. It's not a secret — you should know what it does so you understand where the boundaries are.

What Pastor sees

Pastor's 🗂️ Attendance Records Admin page shows every attendance row across the whole church, with rich filters (date range, event type, LC group, member name) and a paginated table. Each row carries source and state badges Pastor uses to tell apart how each row got there:

The badge legend Pastor uses

What Pastor can do from this page

  • View everything — filter by date, event type, LC group, or member name.
  • Delete individual or bulk records — with a multi-line confirmation that shows exactly what's being removed, plus a special safety warning if any of the selected rows are disputed, pending, or pastor-resolved (deleting these would break the audit trail).
  • Resolve disputes — see the dispute reason you wrote, check both sides, and decide either pastor-confirmed (member was right) or pastor-rejected (LCL was right). The decision is permanent and audit-logged.
  • Audit-log everything — Pastor can always see who logged a row, when, and what state it's in.

What Pastor will NOT do (the boundary)

Pastor's admin tool exists to fix mistakes, not to override your shepherding. As a rule:

  • Pastor doesn't second-guess your normal attendance logs unless something is clearly wrong (a duplicate, an obvious date typo, a member marked present who told Pastor they were sick that day).
  • Pastor doesn't delete disputed rows without a decision — the dispute queue is meant to be resolved, not skipped past.
  • Pastor doesn't backfill weeks of attendance to "make the numbers look better." The numbers are what they are.
A word from the pastor's chair

Kapatid, your work as an LCL is the trustworthy ground this whole system stands on. The admin tool is not a verdict over you — it's a small set of scissors for the rare moments when a row needs to be quietly trimmed. Most weeks Pastor doesn't open it at all. Keep logging faithfully; that's the whole thing.

Reference Glossary

Words and terms you'll see in MLT and throughout this manual.

Batch
A small class of disciples taking the same lesson together over time. Same thing as a cohort.
Backfill (attendance)
An attendance row added after the meeting day — for example, when you forgot to log Thursday's LC night until Saturday. Always allowed for LCLs; the row carries a ⏪ backfill source badge so Pastor can tell late entries apart from same-day ones.
BTLI
Bible Training and Leadership Institute. The structured discipleship curriculum used in our church.
Cohort
The technical name for a batch. Same meaning, just a more formal word.
Confirmation (attendance)
The LCL's action of verifying a member's self-attest. Closes the two-witness loop. Without it, the self-attest stays pending and may not count in all reports.
Disciple
A person you shepherd and help grow in their walk with Christ.
Dispute (attendance)
The LCL's action of objecting to a member's self-attest. The disputed row goes to Pastor for review rather than being silently rejected.
EOLO
Each One Love One — our church's campaign to invite and disciple one person at a time.
FATS
Faithful, Available, Teachable, Selfless — the four qualities we look for in someone ready for leadership.
Forming (batch status)
The batch exists but hasn't started yet. You can still add and remove members.
Active (batch status)
The batch is currently running — meetings are happening, attendance is being logged.
Graduated (batch status)
The batch has finished its curriculum. All members have completed the program.
LCG (LC Group)
Your Life Community group — the larger circle of people you shepherd. A batch is usually a subset of your LCG.
LCL
LC Leader — that's you, if you're reading this manual.
LeaderScope
The set of members you have permission to see and manage — your discipleship tree, ministry team, etc.
Nudge
A gentle reminder from member to LCL (or auto-fired by the system) that a self-attested attendance is waiting for confirmation. Sent via WhatsApp. Limited to once per hour and three times per event.
Pastor's discipler_id
The field on a member's record that says who their direct shepherd is. The Pending Confirmations card filters by this — you only see self-attestations from members whose discipler_id is you.
Pending Confirmation
A self-attested attendance row that's awaiting your confirm-or-dispute decision. Shown on your MLT home screen.
Pipeline Level (L0–L5)
Where someone is on the leadership pipeline: L0 (Pre-Pipeline / new convert), L1 (Lead Yourself), L2 (Lead Others), L3 (Lead Leaders), L4 (Lead a Department), L5 (Lead the Organization / Pastor).
Recovery banner (attendance)
The orange banner that appears when MULTIPLY restored unsaved checks from a previous session — your phone died or the tab got killed before you tapped Save. Tap Save when ready to commit them.
Program
A curriculum like "BTLI 1" or "Pre-Believer's Class." Pastor creates programs; LCLs create batches under those programs.
Cross-level (program)
A program with no specific pipeline level requirement — anyone, regardless of L0/L1/L2 status, can be enrolled.
Self-attest
A member's record of their own attendance, made from MMT. Stays in "pending" state until the LCL confirms or disputes. The complement to LCL-reported attendance.
Source badge (attendance)
The small icon-plus-label Pastor sees on each attendance row indicating where the row came from: 👤 LCL (LCL-logged), 📍 self (member self-attest), 🤝 stand-in (a trusted leader logged it for someone else's group), ⏪ backfill (added after the meeting day), or 🛠 admin (Pastor created or edited directly).
Stand-in attendance
An attendance row logged by a trusted leader on behalf of another group's LCL — e.g., when an LCL is sick and a fellow leader takes the meeting. Marked with the 🤝 stand-in source badge.
State badge (attendance)
A small colored pill Pastor sees next to the present/absent status indicating the row's current self-attest state: 🟡 pending (awaiting LCL confirm), 🟢 confirmed (LCL confirmed), ⚠️ disputed (LCL disputed — awaiting Pastor's call), ✓ pastor-confirmed or ✕ pastor-rejected (Pastor resolved). Rows with no badge are ordinary LCL-logged attendance.
Two-witness model
The MULTIPLY pattern where both the member (self-attest) and the LCL (confirm) can testify to the same attendance event. Mirrors 2 Corinthians 13:1.
Walk-in (ministry attendance)
A substitute or guest who served at a ministry event but isn't on the regular ministry roster. Added inline during ministry attendance. Gets credit for that single event without being permanently added to the team.
Withdraw
To remove a member from a batch while preserving their history. Used when someone leaves a batch before finishing.
