IntroductionWhy MULTIPLY Exists

MULTIPLY is not a database. It is a tool to help you do the one thing Jesus asked His church to do — make disciples — and to do it as a culture, not just a program.

The heart of it — read this first

Everything in MULTIPLY exists to help you shepherd people better — to notice who needs a visit, an encouragement, or a next step before they quietly drift. It is a shepherd's staff, not a spy's camera. The numbers you'll see are prompts for love, never verdicts on a person. If a leader ever uses this tool to shame, rank, or pressure someone, the tool is being misused. We'll come back to this — it matters that much.

1. The vision: a disciple who looks like Jesus

Our target is one verse: "A student who is fully trained will be like their teacher" (Luke 6:40). A fully-trained disciple has two marks — character that looks like Jesus (the fruit of the Spirit, Galatians 5:22–23: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control) and competence to serve and lead at whatever level God has gifted them.

The goal is that this becomes the air the church breathes — so visible that even a newcomer notices it, and so simple that even our children can say what we're about: everyone is being discipled, and everyone is learning to disciple others.

2. The 3Ms — what makes a disciplemaking church

We believe a church multiplies disciples when three things are right:

  • Right Mission — the Great Commission. Make disciples, not just attenders.
  • Right Model — a clear 5-level leadership pipeline so every person can see where they are and what's next.
  • Right Method — EOLO (Each One, Love One): life-on-life disciplemaking, relationship by relationship.

3. The journey: GRACE and the pipeline

Every person in the church is somewhere on one journey — from far from God, all the way to a multiplying leader. We name the stages with the word GRACE, and they line up with the 5-level leadership pipeline (adapted from Mac Lake's The Multiplication Effect).

The disciple's journey: GRACE → the 5-level pipeline

Before anyone advances past Level 1, we look for FATS character — Faithful, Available, Teachable, Selfless. It's not a one-time test; it's the ongoing heartbeat we watch for at every level.

4. The heart: shepherd, don't spy

MULTIPLY will show you patterns you couldn't see before — who hasn't gathered with the body in a while, who's quietly thriving, who might be ready for a next step. That visibility is a gift for love, and it can be misused. So hold these three commitments, and teach your leaders to hold them too:

A leader's covenant with the data

1. It's a prompt, not a verdict. A low number is an invitation to reach out, never a label on a soul. Only God sees the heart (1 Samuel 16:7).
2. It moves you toward people, not away. The right response to a concern is a message, a visit, a prayer — not a judgment whispered to someone else.
3. It stays in the family. What you see here is pastoral trust. Guard it the way you'd want yours guarded.

Used this way, MULTIPLY does what a good shepherd does — it helps you leave the ninety-nine and go after the one (Luke 15:4). That's the whole point.

The heart of MULTIPLYThe Giving Journey — Landas ng Pagtitiwala

Before you use any part of MULTIPLY that touches giving, read this chapter, kapatid. Not because the buttons are hard — but because this is the one part of the system that can be turned into its exact opposite by a good pastor with a good heart. The reasons it is built the way it is are written here so you never have to wonder.

The one sentence to carry

The Giving Journey is not a ladder of amounts. It is a path of trust — Landas ng Pagtitiwala — because giving is the visible edge of an invisible faith in God's provision. We are discipling hearts, not collecting money.

1. Money is a heart issue, not a money issue

Scripture never treats money as a neutral thing to be managed. It treats it as a rival god (Matthew 6:24) and a mirror of the heart — "where your treasure is, there your heart will be also" (Matthew 6:21). So the giving of money is never merely generosity. It is faith (do I believe God will provide?), trust, gratitude (all I hold was first given to me — 1 Chronicles 29:14), accountability, and the recognition that I am a steward, not an owner. That is why we call it a journey of trust, and why every rung speaks of the heart, never of a peso figure.

2. You set the path — the disciple gives the yes

Here is the knot every discipler feels, and how MULTIPLY holds it. Picture a parent and a child:

A parent who says "go to school whenever you want" has abandoned the child — the young one doesn't yet know enough to set his own curriculum. So the path, the pace, the daily walk to the gate — those are yours, pastor. A shepherd who will not call is no shepherd.

But the same parent cannot reach into the child's chest and install the desire to learn. He can require the walk to school; he can only woo the love of learning. In the same way:

The line that governs everything

Obedience can be required. Faith cannot be installed.
You own the invitation — issue it loudly, personally, by name, and keep issuing it. What stays the disciple's, because it cannot be otherwise, is the heart's yes. Not "will you grow" (that is not his to refuse) — but "am I ready to open my hand, and open this journey to you." The most authoritative discipler alive still cannot believe on another man's behalf.

This is why the software never sends the invitation for you and never enrolls anyone automatically. A shepherd's call should not arrive as a notification. You may invite as hard as you like. The system may not press at all — because a "yes" the system manufactured is a tax, and "God loves a cheerful giver… not under compulsion" (2 Corinthians 9:7).

3. Accountability is not surveillance — and secrecy is not the safeguard

You may wonder: shouldn't giving be secret? "Do not let your left hand know what your right hand is doing" (Matthew 6:3). Yes — but look closely at what Jesus forbade. He forbade the trumpet in the synagogue: giving in order to be seen, performed for applause. He did not forbid giving being witnessed in love. He Himself sat and watched the treasury and praised the widow out loud (Mark 12:41–44); Paul organized a public collection and wrote the Macedonians' sacrifice into a letter read aloud to churches (2 Corinthians 8:1–5).

Confession is not performance

A disciple who chooses to open his giving to his mentor is not blowing a trumpet. He is handing someone the authority to ask him hard questions — one of the bravest things a growing believer can do. The safeguard was never secrecy. It is consent: the openness is always his gift to give, never the platform's to extract. For the one who volunteers it, shame and secrecy have no place — he may share the real number, because between two men walking together, honesty is the point.

4. The wall between discipleship and audit

MULTIPLY never reads the church finance office's actual giving records. This is permanent, and the reason is not privacy — it is that a man telling his mentor his giving and an institution checking his receipts are two different acts. One is confession; it disciples. The other is audit; it polices. The moment the app knows what a man actually gave, his leader holds leverage over his wallet, and a covenant of trust becomes a compliance report. So the wall stands: what a disciple shares, he shares — it is never checked against a ledger behind his back.

5. The journey — six steps of trust

Each rung is anchored in the grace texts of 2 Corinthians 8–9, never in Malachi's storehouse threat (the proof-text of every manipulative giving campaign). Jesus affirmed the tithe (Matthew 23:23) even as He called justice and mercy the weightier things — so the tithe is a milestone here, not a law.

  • First Fruits — I gave on purpose. A planned gift, not leftovers. (Proverbs 3:9)
  • Rhythm — Giving is a habit, not a mood. Set aside first, before life eats the rest. (1 Corinthians 16:2)
  • Proportional — I know my percentage, and I chose it. The widow's-mite reversal — the heart is measured, not the wallet.
  • The Tithe — Ten percent is no longer a strain; it's a starting line. (Matthew 23:23)
  • Sacrificial — My giving has begun to cost me something. Where trust becomes real. (2 Corinthians 8:2–3)
  • A Generous Life — Money is downstream of a changed heart — hospitality, the poor, the stranger, EOLO. (2 Corinthians 9:11)

The path is visible to everyone from the start — like the school gate, simply there every morning. It is the pace and the timing you shepherd, never the visibility. And the language is always formation, not completion: "Trust is being formed in you," never "Giving: done." No one graduates from learning to trust God.

6. How a disciple steps on

You issue the call, in your own words: "This is the path. I've walked it. First Fruits is the first step — let me walk it with you." Then the disciple gives one freely-made yes, in two parts: "I'm stepping onto this path," and "I'm walking it openly with you," naming the one leader he invites to walk beside him. That opens a covenant — he may share his real percentage, the leader walks with him and prays and shares his own story, and it is his to close at any time, with no penalty and no notice to anyone. The door he opened, he can also shut. That freedom is what keeps it a gift and not a trap.

7. What MULTIPLY will never do — and why that protects you too

These guardrails are not settings — they are the doctrine

· No dashboard, no leaderboard, no ranking of who gives what. "Six of sixty on Sacrificial" is a comparison that will never exist — the silence about the other fifty-four would become a verdict on them.
· No alert, no AI flag for a low or zero percentage. Ever. Giving is never watched — only ever shared, by the member's own hand.
· A leader can never open a covenant himself, and never ask why it isn't open. The moment you can ask, it stops being a gift.
· The 0% member is invisible to the system. Someone will have a hard season — a lost job, a sick spouse, debt. The app tells no one. You learn it the old way, by asking how he's doing, and you answer it the Acts 4 way: give to him, don't measure him.

8. A pastor's examination of conscience

Before you use any part of the Giving Journey, ask yourself:

Five questions

1. Am I issuing an invitation, or applying a pressure? The first disciples; the second manufactures a tax.
2. Am I prepared to walk the road I'm inviting them onto? You go first — share your own journey, the struggle before the reward.
3. Would I be tempted to look if I could? The guardrails protect you as much as the member. Trust the walls.
4. Have I confused where a person is with who they are? A man at 2% and a man at 50% are on the same road at different mile-markers. Shame has no place in the process.
5. When a member's number is low, is my instinct to measure, or to shepherd? Acts 4 — give to him; do not count him.

Used this way, the Giving Journey does what a good shepherd does: it walks a brother down a hard and beautiful road, at his own pace, with someone beside him — because it takes much to begin, and more to mature, and no one matures alone.

Getting StartedYour First Day with MULTIPLY

No technical skill needed, kapatid. If you can open a web page and tap a button, you can use MULTIPLY. This chapter walks you through it slowly.

The one address to remember

Everything lives at one web address: gejable1.github.io/multiply. Open it in any browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge) on a phone, tablet, or computer. Bookmark it so you never have to type it again.

1. What you need

  • A phone, tablet, or computer.
  • An internet connection.
  • Your name in the church list (your pastor or LC leader adds you), and a 4-digit PIN you'll set the first time.

That's all. There's nothing to download from an app store — though you can add it to your home screen like an app (Section 4).

2. The three tools — and who each is for

MULTIPLY is really three tools behind one front door. You log in once, and you're shown the tool(s) for your role. Most people only ever use one.

The very first time

  1. Open the address

    Go to gejable1.github.io/multiply in your browser.

  2. Find your name

    Start typing your name and pick yourself from the list.

  3. Create your PIN

You can add MULTIPLY to your phone's home screen so it opens like a normal app, full-screen, with one tap.

  1. Open it in Chrome (Android) or Safari (iPhone)

    Go to the tool you use most (members: the Member Tool; leaders: the Leader Tool).

  2. Open the browser menu

    Android Chrome: tap the three dots (⋮). iPhone Safari: tap the Share button (the square with an arrow).

  3. Tap "Add to Home screen" / "Install app"

    Confirm, and an icon appears on your home screen.

Samsung phones — a known quirk

On some Samsung phones, if you've already installed one MULTIPLY app (say the Member Tool) and try to install a second (the Leader Tool), Samsung may say "already installed" and show the wrong icon. The fix: uninstall the first one, install the new one, then reinstall the first. A small annoyance, but it works every time.

5. Finding your way around (for pastors, in MD)

When you open the Dashboard (MD), the menu down the side (or the ☰ button on a phone) is how you move between areas — reports, the member list, interventions, the Vitality report, and Settings (where the Ministry Recommender and SVI are configured). If you ever feel lost, scroll back to the top and use that menu to start fresh.

6. Getting help

Chapter 1 · 🔒 PastorThe Ministry Recommender

The Ministry Recommender looks at what God has wired into a person — their spiritual gifts, personality, and strengths — and suggests which of your church's ministries fit them best. It's a matchmaker between people and places to serve.

Posture first

A recommendation is a starting conversation, not an assignment. The Spirit places people in the body (1 Corinthians 12:18); the tool just helps you see possibilities you might have missed. Always pair it with prayer and a real conversation.

1. What it does

When you open a member's record in MD and tap 🎯 Get AI Recommendations, MULTIPLY produces a "Ministry Fit" chart — a bar for each ministry your church runs, showing how strong a fit that person is (and where there might be friction).

How a recommendation is produced

The more assessments a member has completed (in their Member Tool), the sharper the recommendation. With no assessments, the chart will be thin — that's a cue to invite them to take a few.

2. The two layers — and the one thing you configure

Under the hood there are two layers, and you only ever touch the second:

  • The archetype library — MULTIPLY publishes a shared set of ministry archetypes (gift-fit profiles for common ministries like Children's, Worship, Outreach). You don't build these; they come ready.
  • Your ministries — the actual ministries your church runs, which you already manage in Settings. Your only job is to tell MULTIPLY which archetype each of your ministries is like. That's the "mapping."

Once a ministry is mapped, the recommender knows how to score people for it. Map once; it just works from then on.

3. How to map your ministries

Mapping flow — do this once per ministry
  1. Open Settings → Ministries

    In MD, go to Settings and find the ⛪ Ministries section (Pastor-only).

  2. Edit a ministry (or add one)

    Tap ✏️ Edit on a ministry, or ➕ Add Ministry for a new one.

  3. Choose the Recommender Archetype

    In the edit box you'll see a dropdown labelled Recommender Archetype. Pick the archetype that best matches this ministry (see the reference table below). Leave it on "— Not mapped —" if none fits.

  4. Save

    That's it. The recommender now scores people for that ministry. Repeat for each ministry you run.

5. What "Not mapped" means

If a ministry isn't mapped to an archetype, it simply won't appear in recommendations yet — and the recommender will show a gentle note listing the unmapped ones. No harm done; just edit the ministry and pick an archetype when you're ready.

6. Reading the Ministry Fit chart

  • Green bars are fit signals — reasons this person would thrive there.
  • Red bars are friction signals — places the role might rub against their wiring. (Not disqualifying — just worth a conversation.)
  • The net score ranks the ministries best-fit-first.
  • Tap any bar to see exactly which gifts and traits drove the score, and why.

7. Adding your own archetype (coming soon)

Every church is a little different. If you run a ministry that none of the published archetypes captures, a future update will let you author your own archetype — visible only to your church. For now, pick the closest fit, or leave it unmapped.

Chapter 2 · 🔒 PastorThe Spiritual Vitality Index (SVI)

The SVI is a single 0–100 number for each member that summarizes how engaged their spiritual life looks right now — gathering, the Word, fellowship, mission, growth, and service. It's computed automatically every week from real activity, so you can spot who's flourishing and who may be quietly slipping.

Say it again, because it's the most important thing

The SVI is a care prompt, not a verdict. A low score does not mean someone is a "bad Christian" — only that the visible signals are quiet, and a shepherd should gently check in. Never show a member their number to shame them; never rank people by it. Use it the way a doctor uses a pulse — as a reason to pay attention with love.

1. What it measures: seven dimensions

The score blends seven dimensions of a healthy walk with God:

  • Gather — showing up to worship and to their LC group.
  • Word — time in Scripture (devotionals, reflections).
  • Prayer — a living prayer life (bringing requests, returning to pray, interceding for others, answered prayer).
  • Fellowship — connection with the body.
  • Mission — reaching out (EOLO, evangelism, outreach).
  • Growth — moving forward in the pipeline and lessons.
  • Service — serving in a ministry, leading where called.
How the SVI number is built

The signals it reads

Beyond worship attendance and devotionals, the SVI also watches the quiet, countable signs that someone is being discipled and helping make disciples. Each is measured over a recent window:

SignalWindowDimension
Prayer Meeting attendance8 weeksPrayer
Sunday School attendance8 weeksGather

BTLI lessons passed6 monthsGrowth

Count-first, with a healthy ceiling

Each signal is count-first: MULTIPLY counts what actually happened, then translates it to a 0–10 score. More is better only up to a point — you don't need 20 devotionals to look healthy; a steady rhythm is enough to reach the top of the scale.

2. How the number is built

  1. Each signal becomes a 0–10 score.

    A raw count (or rate) is mapped onto a 0–10 scale by simple thresholds. For example, 1 Prayer Meeting in 8 weeks scores 4; 3 scores 7; 6 scores 10.

  2. The dimensions are blended by weight.

    Each score is multiplied by the weight you gave it for that member's level, then averaged and scaled to a 0–100 total.

  3. “Not applicable” is excused, not punished.

    If a signal doesn't apply to someone — say they're not in any BTLI batch — it's marked n/a and quietly dropped from their score. No one is penalized for a program they're not part of. (Two signals are universal — Prayer Meeting and Celebration — where a zero really is a low signal, so those always count.)

  4. Not enough signal? We say so.

    If there's data on fewer than half of a person's applicable signals, the report shows Insufficient rather than guessing — a fourth state, distinct from Dormant.

  5. Trend.

    Each score is compared to the same person four weeks earlier: ↑ up, → steady, or ↓ down (new if there's no earlier snapshot).

3. What the number means: the three zones

The 0–100 score lands a member in one of three zones. You set the cut-offs, so the words mean what you decide they mean for your church.

The 0–100 scale and its zones (example thresholds)

The 55 and 75 above are only an example — you choose the actual Thriving ≥ and Warming ≥ cut-offs in Settings. Below the Warming line is Dormant.

4. Weights — why level matters

Not every dimension should count the same for every person. A brand-new believer (Level 1) and a seasoned coach (Level 3) are at different places, so MULTIPLY lets you weight the seven dimensions differently per pipeline level. For a new believer you might weight Gather and Word heavily; for a leader you'd add weight to Service and Mission. These weights are set per church and per pipeline level, so every member is scored against their own level's weights.

The weights are relative

The numbers don't need to add up to anything. A weight of 2 simply counts twice as much as a 1. The editor shows each dimension's % share so you can see the balance at a glance.

5. The Weights & Zones editor

  1. Open Settings → ⚖️ SVI Weights & Zones

    This section is Pastor-only.

  2. Pick a pipeline level

    Use the tabs at the top. You're editing the weights for that level.

  3. Adjust the weights

    Raise or lower how much each dimension counts. Watch the % column shift as you do.

  4. Set the zone cut-offs

    Enter Thriving ≥ and Warming ≥. Anything below Warming is Dormant.

  5. Save

    💾 Save stores your changes (they apply at the next weekly recompute). ⚡ Save & Recompute applies them right away to everyone. ↺ Revert undoes unsaved edits.

Go gentle on big changes

Changing weights or cut-offs moves everyone's scores at once. After a "Save & Recompute," some members may shift zones. That's expected — but make changes thoughtfully, not on a whim, so the zones stay meaningful to your leaders.

6. The lookback window

The SVI looks at a recent window of activity (for example, the last several weeks) rather than someone's whole history — so the score reflects how a person is doing lately. Early in a church's life, before there's much history, the window automatically shrinks to whatever data exists, so scores aren't unfairly low just because the system is new.

7. Reading the Member Spiritual Vitality Report

In MD, open ❤️ Member Spiritual Vitality Report. You'll see your people grouped by zone — an at-a-glance picture of who's thriving, who's warming, who's gone quiet, and who has Insufficient data so far. Open any member to see their seven-dimension breakdown, and tap into any dimension to see exactly how each number was derived, in plain words — for example “6 Prayer Meetings attended” or “2 BTLI lessons passed.” Signals that don't apply read “not applicable.” Nothing is hidden; the math is fully transparent, so any number can be explained to the person with a clear conscience.

8. The pastoral bottom line

What the score is for

When you see a member slip into Dormant, the right next move is not a frown — it's a message: "Kumusta ka? Na-miss ka namin." The SVI exists to make sure no one falls through the cracks unnoticed. If it ever becomes a scoreboard instead of a shepherd's tool, turn away from the number and back to the person. That's always the win.

Chapter 3 · 🔒 PastorLoading the Daily Devotional

The devotional is the daily bread your members open in their Member Tool. This chapter shows you how to load it — one day at a time, or a whole month in a single paste.

1. Where it lives

From your Dashboard (MD), open Settings, and under Pastor Tools tap 📖 Devotional Admin. It opens right inside your dashboard (tap ‹ at the top-left to close it). This area is pastor-only.

It's yours alone

Whatever you load here is for your church's members only — each church has its own devotional guide. You're never touching another church's reading, and they're never touching yours.

2. How it reaches your members

Every devotional is tied to a date. When that date arrives, it appears automatically as "Today's devotional" in every member's Member Tool — there is no separate "publish" button to press. Load it ahead of time and it simply shows up on the day. If no entry exists for a date, members just see no reading that day (and the gentle "have you done your devotional?" nudge stays quiet).

3. Two ways to load — pick your moment

At the top you'll see two tabs: 📅 Calendar and 📥 Bulk Paste. Use Calendar to write or fix a single day. Use Bulk Paste to drop in a whole month at once — by uploading a Word or text file or pasting the text.

4. Calendar mode — one day at a time

  1. Open the Calendar tab

    Use ‹ Prev / Today / Next › to land on the right month.

  2. Click the day

    Tap the date square you want to write for. The day editor opens.

  3. Fill what you have

    Really, only the Title and a little body matter — the rest are there to help, not to burden you. The fields: Title, Scripture Reference (e.g. Ecclesiastes 1:1-11 (ESV)), Source Note (optional, e.g. (Rewritten)), Scripture Text (the passage itself), Illustrative Story (an optional opener), Expository Explanation, Response / Application, and Prayer.

  4. Save

    Tap 💾 Save Entry. To remove a day later, reopen it and use 🗑 Delete.

The Weekly Summary field

The last field, 📝 Weekly Summary, is for a Saturday entry that recaps the week and looks ahead to the next. Leave it blank on Monday–Friday entries.

5. Bulk Paste mode — a whole month in one go

If you write (or receive) your devotionals as one document, this is the fast lane. There are two ways in: upload the file, or paste the text.

📄 Easiest: upload the file

Tap 📄 Upload Source File and pick your Word (.docx) or plain-text (.txt) devotional guide. MULTIPLY reads it right there on your phone, fills the box for you, and shows the preview automatically — no copy-pasting. The file is read once and never stored; only the devotional days you actually save are kept. (PDF isn't supported — save it as Word or text first.)

  1. Open the Bulk Paste tab

    Then either tap 📄 Upload Source File and choose your Word/text file, or copy your month's text from Word and paste it into the big box below.

  2. Parse & Preview

    An uploaded file previews on its own. If you pasted instead, tap 🔍 Parse & Preview. Either way the tool reads your document and shows each day it found, so you can check before anything is saved.

  3. Save them all

    Happy with the preview? Choose 💾 Save All (Skip Existing) to add only the dates you haven't filled yet, or ⚠ Save All (Replace Existing) to overwrite days that already have an entry. Tap Discard if something looks off.

The format the parser expects

So the tool can split your document into days, label each one like this, then start the next day with its date on its own line:

May 1, 2025
Title: …
Illustrative Story: …
Passage: …
Expository Explanation: …
Response: …
Prayer: …

6. A gentle rhythm

Stay a step ahead

Because members only see a reading once its date has arrived, load entries before they're needed — a week or a month at a time. An empty "today" is a quiet disappointment; a ready one is a small daily gift.

Why this matters

Daily Bible reading and reflection is the soil everything else grows in. When a member opens the app each morning and finds the Word waiting — a passage, a story, an explanation, a prayer — you are quietly forming the habit that outlasts every program. The devotional isn't a feature to fill; it's a table you set for your people every single day.

GRACE
The five stages of the disciple's journey: Gather → Receive → Anchor → Cultivate → Extend.
Pipeline level
Where someone is in leadership development, L1–L5: Team Member → Leader → Coach → Director → Executive Leader.
FATS
The character we look for before advancing anyone: Faithful, Available, Teachable, Selfless.
EOLO
"Each One, Love One" — the church's relational evangelism and disciplemaking method.
Ministry Recommender
The MD tool that matches a member's gifts and personality to the ministries your church runs.
Archetype
A published gift-fit profile for a common kind of ministry (e.g. "Worship & Music"). You map each of your ministries to one.
Ministry Fit
The chart the recommender produces — a ranked, color-coded picture of how well a person fits each ministry.
SVI · Spiritual Vitality Index
A 0–100 number per member, computed weekly, summarizing how engaged their spiritual life looks lately.
Dimension (SVI)
One of the seven areas the SVI blends: Gather, Word, Prayer, Fellowship, Mission, Growth, Service.
Zone
Which band a member's SVI lands in — Thriving, Warming, or Dormant. You set the cut-offs.
Weight profile
How much each dimension counts toward the SVI — set per pipeline level.
Lookback window
The recent stretch of time the SVI considers, so the score reflects how someone's doing now.
Recompute
Re-running the SVI math for everyone. It happens weekly, or right away with "Save & Recompute."
Batch / Cohort
A small class of disciples taking the same lesson together (managed by LC Leaders in MLT).
